1. What they said

Write the client words. Do not rewrite them as a design.

Problems

Too much contact information. Contact information is spread across different sources, which makes it difficult to manage and find the right contact.

Missing history. Staff may not have a complete record of past interactions or commitments.

Goal

Keep all contact information in one centralized location. Make it easy to search, filter, and manage contacts by geographical area and designation.

Feature requests (client list)

  • Universal contact search. Search and filter phone numbers and contacts by geographical area. Support searches by Assembly Constituency, Block/Tehsil, Gram Panchayat, Village, Panchayat Ward, Municipality, and Municipal Ward.
  • Volunteer registration. Allow people to register as volunteers directly through the application.
  • Appointment and meeting management. Schedule appointments and meetings. Attach relevant meeting documents.
  • Area-wise statistics. Display statistics based on geographical areas. Provide insights into contacts, volunteers, and other relevant data.
  • Travel notifications. Notify people in a particular area when the MLA plans to visit that area.
  • Pin contacts. Users can pin important contacts.
  • Notes section. Every contact has a notes and appointment section.
  • Locals request for MLA (optional). Local people can request development or report issues in their area.

Geographical hierarchy (client list)

Lok Sabha Constituency. Assembly Constituency. Rural: Block/Tehsil, Gram Panchayat, Village, Panchayat Ward. Urban: Municipality, Municipal Ward, Locality/Colony (optional). The geographical hierarchy should support both rural and urban areas. The application should allow users to find contacts at the appropriate administrative level.

Later the same client said: remember it is a POC. Store data in localStorage.

2. Who hurts, when, and the current workaround

Name not given. The PDF names a role, not a person. Do not invent a name.

I think: a staff member in the MLA office hurts. The moment is before a call or a field visit. The staff member must find the right local person by area and by job title.

I think the workaround is: phones, paper lists, WhatsApp groups, and memory. History of talk and promises lives in those same places. It is not in one list.

Public people also hurt when they want to volunteer or to send a local request. I think they use a paper form or a phone call today.

3. Underlying problem

Client confirmed (after the goal review and the order to build):

Contact data lives in many sources. Staff cannot find a person by administrative area in one place. Staff do not have a full record of past talk and past promises.

The client also confirmed the scope for this version: a POC in the browser. No server database. No SMS system.

I think, not fully confirmed: “Notify people” in the PDF means SMS or WhatsApp in a later product. For the POC, an in-app notice can show the same idea. The client accepted a POC. The client did not confirm SMS.

Check: if we only add one filter, the hurt does not stop. Staff still need one list, area levels for rural and urban, and history on each person.

4. What “solved” looks like for the client

Write outcomes. Do not name buttons as success.

  • Staff find a contact by area and by designation from one list. They do not ask a colleague for a number.
  • Staff open a contact and see past notes and meetings before they call.
  • Staff mark important people so those people stay easy to find.
  • A person can register as a volunteer in the application. Staff can see that registration.
  • Staff can schedule a meeting and keep a small meeting file with it.
  • Staff can pick an area and see counts for contacts, volunteers, meetings, and requests in that area and below it.
  • When the MLA plans a visit, staff can publish a notice for that area. People in that area can see the notice in the application.
  • Optional: a local person can send a development or issue request. Staff can mark it as reviewed.

For this POC, “solved” does not mean: login, shared data across computers, or real SMS.

5. Questions to confirm

These questions can change the plan. Clear asks are still dangerous. Write the questions.

  1. Who is the named staff owner of the contact list? Give a name, not a role.
  2. For the first version, does “notify” mean an in-app notice only, or SMS / WhatsApp?
  3. How many Lok Sabha and Assembly units must the first tree hold? One sample tree, or the full constituency?
  4. Must Locality/Colony exist in the first urban path, or can it stay optional?
  5. Is “Locals request for MLA” in the first build or out of it?
  6. What file types and size must a meeting document support?

Answers we already have: POC. localStorage. Rural and urban both in. Optional local requests in the POC. In-app travel notices. Meeting files under 400 KB in the browser.

6. Blast radius

If this work is wrong, the damage is not one screen.

  • Direct: contact list, search, pin, notes, meetings.
  • One hop: geography tree. If the tree is wrong, search, statistics, and travel notices all fail.
  • One hop: volunteer list and public volunteer form. Same area model.
  • One hop: local requests. Same area model. Staff inbox.
  • One hop: staff screens vs public screens. A public user must not see staff lists.
  • Storage hop: all lists sit in one browser key. A bad write can wipe sample data. Reset exists for the POC.

This is not a two-hour change on one report. It is a new desk that many flows share.

7. Rough estimate to resolve

Browser POC with sample geography: about 3 to 5 working days for one developer who knows the stack.

This estimate is not for a production system. Production with login, a shared database, and SMS is a later plan. Do not mix that estimate with this POC.
